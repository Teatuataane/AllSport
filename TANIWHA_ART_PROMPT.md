# Taniwha art — ChatGPT draft prompt

Drafts only. See the note at the bottom before any of this becomes final art.

---

## How to use this

ChatGPT image generation cannot produce ten registered layers or a genuinely
transparent PNG, so it cannot make the shippable asset. What it is good for is
**settling what each of the twelve creatures looks like** before anyone spends
time in vector.

1. Paste **Prompt A** once, to set the rules for the whole set.
2. Then paste **Prompt B** twelve times, once per taniwha, swapping in the brief.
   One creature per message — asking for twelve at once produces twelve muddled
   variations of the same thing.
3. Keep the ones that work, then have them redrawn as vector and sliced.

---

## Prompt A — set the rules (paste first)

> I'm designing a set of twelve creature icons for a community sport in
> Ōtautahi, Aotearoa. They are taniwha — guardian creatures from Māori
> storytelling, usually described as serpentine or lizard-like water beings.
>
> Every image you make for me in this conversation must follow these rules
> exactly:
>
> **Medium.** A solid black silhouette on a pure white background. No shading,
> no gradients, no outlines, no internal detail lines, no texture, no
> background scenery, no text, no border, no drop shadow. Flat black on flat
> white, nothing else. The final artwork gets recoloured in software, so any
> colour or shading you add has to be thrown away.
>
> **Anatomy — this is the important one.** Every taniwha in the set is built
> from exactly the same eleven pieces, and each piece must be visually separable
> from its neighbours, because the artwork will later be cut apart and stacked
> up one piece at a time:
> head, body, tail, left arm, right arm, left leg, right leg, wings, tongue,
> an implement it holds, and a crown or headdress.
> So: keep the limbs clear of the body outline, don't let the tail cross the
> legs, don't tuck a limb or a wing behind the torso, and hold the implement
> clear of the body in one hand. No coiled or knotted poses.
>
> **Pose.** Standing side-on in profile, facing left, all four limbs visible and
> separated, wings open and clear of the back, tail extended behind, head
> raised, tongue out in a wero — the challenge, like a pūkana — and the
> implement held out in one hand. Same pose for all twelve, so the set reads as
> one family.
>
> **Readability.** It must be recognisable both at 400 pixels and at 24 pixels.
> Chunky, confident shapes. No thin spikes, no lacework, no fine filigree —
> anything under a few pixels wide disappears.
>
> **Distinctiveness.** The twelve differ only in silhouette character — build,
> proportion, the shape of the crown, the line of the tail. They must be
> tellable apart with no colour at all, because colour is applied later.
>
> **Respect.** Draw on Māori design language in the forms — the koru spiral, the
> curved surface patterns of whakairo carving — as shape, not as decoration
> pasted on. Do not copy any specific carving, wharenui, iwi emblem or existing
> logo. These are new creatures, not depictions of named ancestors.
>
> Reply "ready" and I'll give you the first one.

## The twelve briefs

Swap each of these into `{BRIEF}`.

| # | Name | Domain | Brief |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Te Taniwha ō te Whānau** | — (everyone's first) | The origin of the set — the one every player builds first. Balanced, welcoming, none of the extremes of the others. Slightly larger head, open posture, a broad simple crown. It should look like the parent the other eleven descend from. |
| 2 | **Te Taniwha ō te Kaha** | Maximal Strength | Heaviest of the set. Thick neck, deep chest, short powerful limbs, planted stance, low centre of gravity. Blunt heavy crown like a set of horns. Built to lift. |
| 3 | **Te Taniwha ō te Kaha Tinana** | Calisthenics | Strength without bulk. Long lean limbs, wide shoulders, narrow waist, gripping hands and feet. Poised as if about to hang from something. A light swept-back crown. |
| 4 | **Te Taniwha ō te Hiko** | Power (explosive) | Coiled and about to release. Compact loaded body, deeply bent hind legs, tail whipped up in a sharp angle. Crown that reads as a jagged fork, like lightning. |
| 5 | **Te Taniwha ō te Tere** | Speed | The leanest and longest. Stretched low body, slender limbs mid-stride, tail streaming straight out behind. Everything swept backwards. A crown flattened by wind. |
| 6 | **Te Taniwha ō te Manawanui** | Anaerobic Endurance | Enduring under load. Braced and gritted, shoulders hunched, limbs locked and holding a position rather than moving. Thick blocky crown. It should look like it is refusing to stop. |

## The implements — part ten

Each taniwha holds the tool of its own discipline. Every one is taken from a
real event in that domain, which is also how the two disciplines with no obvious
equipment got one: Speed has Beach Flags, and Flexibility's splits are scored as
block height from the ground.

| # | Taniwha | Holds | From |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Whānau | **many hands** reaching out | its crown is a referral, and the sport needs no equipment to start |
| 2 | Kaha | a **barbell** | Deadlift, Clean & Press |
| 3 | Kaha Tinana | a pair of **rings** | Iron Cross, Front Lever |
| 4 | Hiko | a **javelin** | Javelin |
| 5 | Tere | a **flag** | Beach Flags, Capture the Flag |
| 6 | Manawanui | an **ab wheel** | Ab Rollout |
| 7 | Manawaroa | an **oar** | Row Erg, Ski Erg |
| 8 | Ngāwari | a **block** | Forward Split, Middle Split |
| 9 | Mataara | a **jump rope** | Jump Rope |
| 10 | Ruruku | a **racquet** | Tennis, Badminton, Squash |
| 11 | Tika | a **bow** | Archery |
| 12 | Te Kāhui | **the other eleven taniwha**, fanned out behind it | holding all eleven crowned |

Barbell and javelin are the two at risk of reading as a plain bar — draw the
grip and the point deliberately. Ab wheel, oar, jump rope, racquet and bow are
the strongest silhouettes in the set.

## Exporting from Canva

Canva has no "export each layer separately", so the reliable method is one page
per piece. It takes about ten minutes per taniwha and it is the only way to keep
the pieces registered.

**Set the canvas up once.** Create the design as **Custom size, 1000 × 1000 px**.
Not a template, not a resize afterwards — the page dimensions have to be fixed
before you draw, because resizing later moves everything.

**Then, per taniwha:**

1. Draw the whole creature on **one page**, with each piece as its own element or
   group. Get it finished and positioned before you split anything.
2. **Duplicate that page eleven times** (right-click the page → Duplicate). You
   now have eleven identical copies.
3. On page 1, **delete everything except the head**. On page 2, everything except
   the body. And so on, in this order:

   `pane · tinana · hiku · ringa-maui · ringa-matau · waewae-maui · waewae-matau · parirau · arero · {implement} · tikitiki`

4. **Only ever delete. Never move, resize, nudge or re-centre anything.** The
   moment a piece is repositioned, that piece is registered to a different origin
   and the creature will come apart when the app stacks it. Everything must stay
   exactly where it sat on the finished drawing.
5. **Download → PNG → tick "Transparent background" → select all 11 pages.**
   Transparent background is a Canva Pro feature; without it every piece exports
   on a white square and the app will tint the whole square, not the shape.
   Leave "Size" at 1× — what matters is that all eleven match, not how large.
6. Canva names them `Design name - 1.png`, `- 2.png` … Rename to the slugs in
   step 3 and drop them in `public/taniwha/{taniwha-slug}/`.

**Colour does not matter.** The app uses the artwork as a *mask*: it reads only
the alpha channel and fills the shape with the domain colour. So draw in black,
or in the domain colour, or in hot pink — it all renders the same. What does
matter is that the fill is **flat and fully opaque**. Any shading, gradient or
partial transparency comes through as a partly-tinted patch.

## Check the export before you draw the next one

```
node scripts/check-taniwha-art.mjs kaha
```

It verifies all eleven pieces are present, all the same canvas, square, large
enough, and carrying a real alpha channel — then writes
`public/taniwha/_preview.html`, which shows the taniwha assembling one piece at
a time, masked and tinted exactly as the app does it.

```
open public/taniwha/_preview.html
```

**Watch for a piece that jumps, shifts or changes scale between frames.** That is
the registration failure, and it is the one thing that cannot be fixed
afterwards — it means the pieces were cropped individually rather than exported
from one shared canvas. Everything else can be redrawn later; that has to be
caught now. Run it on the first taniwha before drawing the other eleven.

Folders, one per taniwha:

```

manawaroa  ngawari  mataara  ruruku  tika  kahui
```

Files inside each, in assembly order:

```
pane  tinana  hiku  ringa-maui  ringa-matau
waewae-maui  waewae-matau  parirau  arero  tikitiki

# plus part ten, the implement — named for the tool, one per taniwha:
hands  barbell  rings  javelin  flag  ab-wheel
oar  block  jump-rope  racquet  bow  taniwha
```

A filename that is not the exact slug **falls back silently** and draws nothing,
the same trap the event icons have. The checker catches this too.

## Before this becomes final art

Two things worth deciding deliberately rather than by default.

**The names are not settled.** Four of the ten domain words are placeholders —
Hiko, Manawanui, Mataara and especially Ruruku — and are waiting on a reo
review. Drawing to a name that then changes wastes the work, so the reo review
is worth finishing first.

**Taniwha are not a generic fantasy creature.** They belong to Māori
storytelling, and AllSport is a Māori kaupapa. Using an AI image model to
*explore* what twelve creatures might look like is a reasonable way to think
out loud. Shipping AI-generated taniwha as the permanent visual identity of the
sport is a different decision, and one a Māori artist — ideally one with a
connection to Ōtautahi — should have a hand in. The drafts are cheap; the
identity is not.
